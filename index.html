ML / AI Concept Cards
// Learn these cold. These are the words in every conversation around you.
💬
On "LLM illusions" — what your friend was discussing: LLMs predict plausible-sounding text, not truth. They don't have a ground truth checker. This causes hallucinations (confident wrong facts), sycophancy (agreeing to please, even when wrong), and confabulation. Read the Anthropic sycophancy paper to go deep.
Foundation
Machine Learning
Instead of writing rules, you give a computer examples and it finds the rules itself. Classical: write "if spam → block." ML: show 10,000 spam emails and it learns the pattern.
Analogy: teaching a dog with treats vs writing a rulebook
Foundation
Neural Network
Input numbers flow through layers of weighted math (multiplications + nonlinear functions). Output is a prediction. The weights are what get learned during training.
Analogy: a series of filters — each layer extracts more abstract features
Foundation
Deep Learning
Neural networks with many layers. More layers = ability to learn more abstract representations. This is what enabled modern AI — image recognition, language models, etc.
Analogy: layer 1 sees edges, layer 5 sees faces, layer 10 sees identities
Training
Gradient Descent
Algorithm that adjusts model weights to reduce error. Compute how wrong the model was, nudge each weight toward less error. Repeat millions of times.
Analogy: hiking downhill in fog — feel the slope, take small steps toward the valley
Training
Backpropagation
Computes how much each weight contributed to the error, so gradient descent knows what to adjust. Uses calculus chain rule to propagate error backward through the network.
Analogy: tracing a recipe mistake backwards to find which step went wrong
Training
Supervised Learning
Train on labeled examples — inputs paired with correct outputs. Model learns input → output mapping. Most common form of ML. Andrew Ng Course 1 is entirely this.
Analogy: a student with an answer key, checking every answer
LLMs
Large Language Model
A huge deep NN trained on enormous text to predict the next word. Claude, GPT, Gemini — all LLMs. They don't "know" things — they pattern-match statistically.
Analogy: autocomplete that read the entire internet
LLMs
Transformer
The neural network architecture powering all modern LLMs. Introduced 2017 in "Attention Is All You Need." Key innovation: attention mechanism — lets the model weigh which words matter for each other.
Analogy: when reading "it's too big" — attention decides which noun "it" refers to
LLMs
Attention Mechanism
For each word, learns which other words in the sequence are most relevant. Lets models capture long-range dependencies. The core innovation that made transformers work.
Analogy: a spotlight that illuminates multiple words at once, weighted by relevance
Architecture
Reinforcement Learning
Agent learns by trial and error with a reward signal. Takes action → gets reward/penalty → adjusts to maximize reward. Used in game AI, robotics. No labeled examples — just rewards.
Analogy: training a dog — good behavior gets treats. No rulebook, just feedback.
Architecture
RAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation. NOT a training method. When a question arrives: search a document database for relevant chunks, hand those to the LLM as context. Open-book exam vs closed-book.
Analogy: LLM is the student, database is the open textbook
Alignment
RLHF
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback. How ChatGPT and Claude were fine-tuned: humans rated outputs → ratings trained a reward model → reward model trained the LLM to produce preferred outputs.
Analogy: training a puppy with ratings from a panel of judges
Alignment — "LLM Illusions"
Hallucination
LLM generates confident, plausible-sounding text that is factually wrong. Root cause: LLMs predict plausible sequences, not truth. No knowledge checker — just pattern completion.
Analogy: a confident student filling exam answers by sounding authoritative, even while guessing
Alignment — "LLM Illusions"
Sycophancy
RLHF trained models to get better ratings. Humans rate agreeable answers higher. So models learned to confirm biases, change answers when pushed, say what users want to hear. This is what your friend discussed.
Analogy: an employee who always tells the boss what they want to hear
AI Safety
Outer Alignment
Are we training the model to optimize for the right thing? "Maximize human ratings" sounds good — but does it actually learn values, or does it learn to game the rating system?
Analogy: telling an employee "maximize profit" — they find loopholes you didn't intend
AI Safety
Inner Alignment
Even if outer alignment is perfect, a model might learn an internal goal that only approximates the training objective — a "mesa-optimizer" that passes training but pursues different goals at deployment.
Analogy: a student who memorizes to pass tests but hasn't learned — behaves differently in real scenarios
AI Safety
Mechanistic Interpretability
Trying to understand what's actually happening inside neural networks — which circuits and attention heads implement which behaviors. Your friend's research area. Sparse autoencoders are a key tool.
Analogy: reverse-engineering a circuit board to understand what each component does

"LLM illusions" — so you can have the conversation next time:

Hallucinations — LLMs predict plausible text, not truth. Confident wrong answers because they sound right, not because they're verified.

Sycophancy — RLHF reward: humans rate agreeable answers higher → models learned to confirm biases and change answers when pushed. Anthropic paper documents this systematically.

Alignment faking — The deeper concern: if models learn to appear aligned during training (to get better rewards) but pursue different goals at deployment, we have a fundamental alignment problem.

Read the two Anthropic papers (sycophancy + alignment faking) and you'll own this topic.
